## Research-backed replacement directions

These are proposals for real independent builds, not invented past engagements. The recommendation is a catalogue/data pipeline plus a document-to-data workspace, alongside Signal. A cross-system operations integration is a strong alternative to either, depending on the clients the owner wants.

### A. Catalogue/data pipeline

Business situation: a retailer or marketplace receives inconsistent product data from multiple sources.

Demonstrable experience: import two differently shaped source feeds, map their fields, identify duplicates and variants, review missing data, inspect a proposed cleaned product beside its sources, then approve a destination-ready export. A repeated run should recognise previously processed records and isolate changed inputs. If enrichment uses AI, it must be a real server-side adapter with inspectable output and a bounded cost policy; a fixture must remain labelled as a fixture.

Why it fits: builds on Python/automation/data integration and creates an understandable before/after. It complements Signal's research with the harder step of turning source material into reliable operational data.

Independent demonstration boundary: owned/sample feeds and synthetic products; no unapproved site scraping, live shop publishing or fabricated client success metrics.

### B. Document-to-data workspace

Business situation: a team repeatedly copies information from scanned documents into spreadsheets or an internal system.

Demonstrable experience: import a new sample PDF/image, run real OCR/extraction, show each field beside the relevant page/region, flag uncertainty, correct a value, validate the final record, then export structured data. Include an unfamiliar-layout fixture so the demonstration is more than one hardcoded form. Use low-risk sample product sheets or delivery documents instead of pretending to provide legal/tax conclusions.

Why it fits: gives clients an immediate, observable transformation and demonstrates frontend, backend and model/OCR integration in one coherent product.

Independent demonstration boundary: original or licensed sample documents, no private user documents and no invented extraction accuracy. Provider/API costs and storage need concrete implementation decisions before integration.

### C. Cross-system operations integration

Business situation: supplier price lists, stock records and a store do not agree.

Demonstrable experience: load mismatched source snapshots, detect drift, apply mappings and business rules, show a proposed change set, resolve conflicts, approve a dry-run batch and export a reconciliation report. Prove repeated runs do not duplicate updates and incomplete product records are held for review.

Why it fits: grounded in the user's actual API/Linux/automation experience and similar in depth to the retailer integration case study. Demonstrates making existing tools work together, not asking clients to abandon established platforms.

Independent demonstration boundary: clearly simulated external adapters until a real sandbox connection is configured. No financial or store writes without explicit approval and a dry-run receipt.
